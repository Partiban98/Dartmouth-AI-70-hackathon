## What the judges said they score (their words, not ours)

- The theme is one question: **what must remain human?** The video must open with the sentence "In our project, a person always ___."
- Three judge questions, said twice: **Who is this for? What could go wrong? Does it help people or decide for them?**
- "We're looking for more of the idea, the why behind it, versus the plumbing and infrastructure of your app. The idea is really what matters."
- "Call out why it matters, what are some of the risks or consequences, and what remains human throughout."
- Think about the negatives too: power concentration, autonomy, trust.
- The organizer's own example: Claude did his taxes well, "but you have to double check. Who answers for Claude if he's doing the wrong math?"
- Judges: Anthropic staff plus faculty. Prizes: 1st Claude Max for half a year + $750 + 30 min with an Anthropic expert.
- Submission: team name, members, track, project name, one-sentence description, video link (2.5 min max), optional code or live demo link.

## The one-line answer we already have

**"In our project, a person always assigns the grade."** The receipt on screen already says "Assigned by you (TA)". Open with the sentence, close with the receipt. Everything between is proof.

## Why this beats the room

Most teams in that room are building "AI that does X for you" (the team next to us pitched the same adaptive-learning platform we started with; that is why we pivoted to grading). Remain is the opposite: **AI that cannot do the human part even if you ask it to.** The model never sees a point value, never computes, never writes a grade; the grade is a text box a named person fills in; the log records who. Our answer to "who answers for Claude" is: a person, by name, every time, and here is the log.

That is not a feature claim, it is a design constraint, and we can show it in 10 seconds: the model's JSON has three fields, code, quote, confidence. Nothing else counts.

## The second thing nobody else will have: a real rubric and real precedents, owned by the instructor

Partiban and Kishore graded 94 real papers this month. Quiz 3 Q5 had three valid methods and a rubric with one. The precedents the TAs and the professor agreed on ("a p-value range containing the exact value earns full credit", "Wald SE is a variant, no deduction", "a wrong decision that follows from the student's own wrong number is not a second error") are now a versioned, visible table in the app, validated by code. That is the answer to **power concentration**: the rules are not in one grader's head or one model's weights; they are on screen, versioned, and owned by the instructor. Say that sentence in the video.

Honesty rule for this data: real rubric, real precedents, real TA outcomes (anonymized at source, no names, no images); paper text in the ENGS 93 set is reconstructed from the TA's notes and is labelled as such on screen. The live loop in the video runs on the synthetic DEMO-STATS 101 papers.

## Video script, 2:27, three judge questions as the three acts

0:00–0:08 **Opening, a teammate on camera, not a slide:** "In our project, a person always assigns the grade."

0:08–0:35 **Who is this for.** Partiban or Kishore, in one breath: "I'm a first-time TA. Two weeks ago I graded Quiz 3. Question 5 had three valid methods; the rubric had one. Students who used a different method got different marks depending on who graded. The same mistake should cost the same mark, and a person should still be the one who decides." Cut to the rubric panel: codes, deductions, listed methods, "Validated by code: OK".

0:35–1:25 **What could go wrong, each with its safeguard on screen.** Narrate while clicking through S-07 live:
- "The model could leak a student's name to the cloud." Gate panel on S-12: NAME 1 stripped, counts only, alias-only payload, approve button. (5 s)
- "The model could invent evidence." Review card: every quote says "verified in source" or "not found in source", checked by code, never trusted from the model. (8 s)
- "The model could grade." Score line: computed only from codes the TA confirmed; the model's JSON has no points field. (8 s)
- "The TA could rubber-stamp." No bulk approve, one paper at a time, override needs a reason; override count on screen. (8 s)
- "Whoever owns the rules owns the class." Rubric is versioned and instructor-owned; the precedents are visible. (8 s)
- The correction: S-07 used a valid method the rubric did not list. The rule flags it, the TA overrides it, types the reason, assigns 10. Receipt: "Assigned by you (TA): Partiban". (13 s)

1:25–1:55 **Does it help people or decide for them.** Class insights from TA-confirmed decisions only: "E-VAR-SD confirmed 7 times, so Monday we reteach variance vs SD." Then the real set: 38 real papers, real rubric, insights from the TA's own decisions. "The model proposed; the person decided; the class gets the same mark for the same mistake."

1:55–2:15 **What remains human, stated plainly over the receipt and the log.** "The instructor owns the rules. The TA confirms or overrides every call and assigns every grade. The student gets consistency. The model proposes with evidence and is named on screen: LIVE API: claude-sonnet-5-5. It is not local, and we do not claim it is."

2:15–2:27 **Close.** Metrics strip from logs: proposed, confirmed, overridden, quotes verified vs not found, identifiers stripped by type. "Remain. Show your work." Team card.

Muted test: every beat ends on a receipt or a count. Captions throughout.
